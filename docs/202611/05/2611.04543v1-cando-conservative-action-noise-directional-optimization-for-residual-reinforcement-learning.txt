# CANDO: Conservative Action-Noise Directional Optimization for Residual Reinforcement Learning 

Zeyu Gao 1,2∗, Chengkai Hou 3∗, Chaoyue Li 4, Zhuangzhe Wu 3,4, Jiaming Liu 3,4,Yao Mu 5, Peng Jia 4, Li Zhang 1,2† and Shanghang Zhang 3†

Abstract — Pretrained generative policies provide strong be-havior priors for robot control, but adapting them efficiently with online reinforcement learning remains difficult. A central challenge is how to improve a frozen generative policy without destroying the structure learned from offline demonstrations. Noise-space residual methods address this by modifying the policy’s input noise, but existing noise-space methods typically regulate adaptation through fixed latent feasible regions, which may exclude valuable noise edits. In contrast, action-space residual methods are more sample-efficient, but their efficiency can still be further improved. We propose Conservative Noise Directional Optimization (CNDO), a noise-space residual RL method that improves a frozen generative policy by learning state- and noise-conditioned directions around Gaussian latent samples. CNDO removes predefined latent feasible regions and instead regulates noise-space adaptation through the soft conservative constraint, which consists of conservative value estimation and gated execution. We further introduce Conservative Action-Noise Directional Optimization (CANDO), which adds an action-space residual, combining structured latent-space improvement with local action correction. Experiments show that, once CNDO narrows the gap between noise-space and action-space residual learning, CANDO further improves over single-space optimization, achieving 76.1 NAUC and a 91.4% final success rate, surpassing the best single-space results by 6.6 NAUC and 4.1% success rate. More demos and info are available on https://cando-rl.github.io/. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Learning robot policies from offline demonstrations pro-vides an effective way to acquire complex manipulation skills without manually designing task-specific controllers. In particular, generative policies based on diffusion models and conditional flow matching can represent complex and multi-modal action distributions and have demonstrated strong performance across a broad range of robotic tasks [1], [2]. However, behavior cloning is inherently limited by the coverage of demonstration data and is susceptible to covariate shift during closed-loop deployment [3]. This motivates lightweight adaptation methods that keep the pretrained policy frozen while learning only a task-specific adaptation module. Residual reinforcement learning provides a natural frame-work for this setting by keeping a base policy fixed and learning only an additional residual correction [6]. For a frozen generative policy, residual adaptation can operate in two distinct spaces. In noise space, the input noise is modified before generation to alter the behavior produced by the frozen policy; in action space, the generated action is directly corrected after decoding. Diffusion Steering via Reinforce-ment Learning (DSRL) represents the former approach by treating the input noise as the action of a latent-space Markov decision process [5]. In contrast, Distribution Contractive Reinforcement Learning (DICE) learns a lightweight residual over the generated action and combines residual optimization with value-guided action selection [7]. Although both spaces provide viable interfaces for online policy improvement, two important questions remain: can noise-space residual learning achieve sample efficiency comparable to strong action-space residual learning, and can the two spaces be jointly optimized to further improve policy learning? Existing noise-space methods are substantially less sample-efficient than DICE on most of the tasks considered in our experiments. We find that this gap is partly associated with how noise-space policy updates are regulated. DSRL restricts latent actions to a bounded region, Latent Policy Steering (LPS) projects them onto the typical Gaussian hypersphere, and Lagrangian Perturbation Diffusion Steering (LP-DS) retains Gaussian noise samples while constraining their residual perturbations through a Lagrangian trust region [5], [8], [9]. Such mechanisms improve training stability by explicitly limiting the region that the noise policy can explore. However, latent displacement is only indirectly related to the quality of the behavior produced after decoding. Consequently, a predefined geometric region may reject large but valuable latent edits while still admitting small edits that do not improve the resulting behavior. This suggests that noise-space adaptation should be regulated according to the quality of the decoded behavior, rather than solely by a predefined geometric region in latent space. Re-moving hard geometric restrictions enables broader noise-space exploration, while increasing the risk of unstable optimization. CNDO introduces a soft conservative constraint consisting of two components: a conservative critic and gated execution. The conservative critic suppresses unsupported value overestimation when optimizing noise directions, while the gate selectively executes proposed edits according to target-critic value estimates. In this way, CNDO expands the effective optimization range in noise space while preventing unrestricted exploration from destabilizing policy learning. We further combine CNDO with action-space residual learning and introduce Conservative Action-Noise Direc-tional Optimization (CANDO) . The two residual branches affect the final policy through different pathways: the noise-space residual modifies the behavior generated through the frozen policy, whereas the action-space residual directly corrects the generated action. However, having access to both adaptation spaces does not necessarily guarantee additional gains from joint training. Our experiments show that the benefit of joint learning depends on the capabilities of both residual learners. When their standalone performance differs substantially, combining the two provides only limited improvement over the stronger learner. Once noise-space learning is improved to a level comparable to action-space residual learning, joint training can more effectively exploit policy improvements from both spaces. > •

We identify a mismatch between hard latent-geometric constraints and decoded behavioral quality in noise-space residual learning, and propose CNDO to regulate noise-space adaptation through the soft conservative constraint rather than a fixed latent feasible region.  

> •

We further introduce CANDO , which jointly optimizes CNDO and action-space residual learning and achieves higher overall sample efficiency than the best single-space method.  

> •

A. Residual Reinforcement Learning 

Residual reinforcement learning improves an existing controller or policy by preserving its effective behavioral prior and learning only the corrections that are missing from the base policy. Early works on Residual Policy Learning and Residual Reinforcement Learning for Robot Control add trainable residual actions on top of hand-designed or model-based controllers, allowing reinforcement learning to avoid searching over the entire control policy from scratch [6], [10]. This paradigm was later extended to contact-rich manipulation with visual observations [11], shared autonomy [12], and settings where the base controller is obtained from demonstrations or motion primitives rather than manually designed [13], [14]. 

B. Noise-Space Policy Steering 

Using latent representations to structure policy optimization predates generative robot policies. PLAS optimizes policies in a VAE-derived latent action space, while OPAL and SPiRL learn temporally extended latent primitives or skill priors from offline data [15]–[17]. LAPO further learns a latent-variable policy through advantage-weighted optimization to generate high-value actions [18]. With diffusion policies, the Gaussian noise input provides a native latent interface for modifying generated behavior. To the Noise and Back demonstrates that manipulating the diffusion process can effectively incorporate external guidance in shared autonomy [19]. More closely related to our setting, DSRL formulates the input noise of a frozen diffusion policy as the action of a latent-space MDP and optimizes it using reinforcement learning [5]. LPS directly optimizes a latent actor using gradients from an original-action-space critic, backpropagated through a differentiable one-step MeanFlow policy, and constrains the actor output to the Gaussian hypersphere [8]. More recently, LP-DS retains samples from the Gaussian prior and learns residual noise perturbations while regulating their magnitude through a Lagrangian trust-region constraint [9]. 

C. Action-Space Residual Reinforcement Learning 

In contrast to noise-space steering before policy decoding, action-space residual learning directly corrects the output action. Recent work has extended the classical residual formulation to high-capacity policies pretrained from offline demonstrations. ResiP learns closed-loop per-step residual corrections over frozen action-chunking policies to compen-sate for execution errors in precise assembly tasks [20]. Policy Decorator introduces a model-agnostic residual adapter for on-line refinement of large pretrained policies [21], while ResFiT applies off-policy RL to learn lightweight per-step corrections over behavior-cloning policies [22]. Related approaches also use value functions to coordinate pretrained behaviors with online policy improvement. IBRL treats actions proposed by an imitation policy as candidates for both exploration and temporal-difference bootstrapping [23]. DAWN further studies critic cold-start and scale mismatch between base and residual actions as key bottlenecks for value learning in residual RL [24]. Most closely related to our action-space component, DICE combines residual action optimization with selective behavioral regularization and value-guided multi-candidate action selection, providing a strong sample-efficient action-space baseline [7]. USR unifies noise-space steering and action-space residual refinement within a single actor, jointly optimizing latent noise and residual actions, but does not thoroughly investigate the interactions and complementary roles of these two optimization strategies [25]. A. Conservative Noise Directional Optimization

Existing noise-space methods are shown in Fig. 2. These constraints stabilize policy optimization by explicitly restrict-ing latent exploration. DSRL bounds each latent dimension through a tanh actor, LPS normalizes latent variables onto the typical hypersphere of a high-dimensional Gaussian, and LP-DS combines residual noise perturbations with explicit bounds and a Lagrangian trust-region constraint [5], [8], [9]. Although these constraints improve stability, latent displacement is only an indirect indicator of decoded behavior quality. Large latent changes may produce valuable behaviors, while small changes do not necessarily imply better actions. Geometric constraints may therefore exclude useful noise-induced policy improvements and limit sample efficiency. We summarize CANDO in Alg. 1. CANDO alternates be-tween off-policy parameter updates and online data collection. During each update, we use RLPD [26] to sample states and transitions from a mixture of expert and online replay, and sample fresh Gaussian latents to construct the base behavior

Baseline algorithms and proposed methods. We compare the following frozen-policy adaptation methods: 1) Base policy: the pretrained frozen Flow Matching behavior policy without online adaptation. 2) DSRL [5]: a noise-space method that learns latent inputs for the frozen generative policy. 3) LPS [8]: a latent-policy method that directly propagates action-space value gradients through a differentiable flow decoder while constraining the latent input to the typical Gaussian hypersphere. 4) LP-DS [9]: residual noise steering with an explicit latent bound and a Lagrangian trust-region constraint. 5) CNDO : our noise-space directional learner with conser-vative value estimation and gated execution. 6) DICE [7]: an action-space residual learner with value-guided candidate selection. 7) DSRL+DICE : Combination of DSRL noise learning and DICE action residual learning, following a formulation similar to USR [25]. 8) LP-DS+DICE : combination of LP-DS noise learning and DICE action residual learning. 9) CANDO : our joint CNDO and action-residual learner. 

B. Noise-Space Policy Improvement 

To address Q1 , we compare CNDO with existing noise-space methods and use DICE as the action-space reference. Fig. 4 and Tab. I show that CNDO consistently improves over the frozen policy and is the strongest noise-space method on average. Relative to DSRL, CNDO raises average NAUC from 57.9 to 69.5 and Final SR from 66.2% to 81.5%; relative to LP-DS, the gains are 9.6 NAUC points and 21.0 Final SR points. Relative to the LPS, the gains are 27.8 NAUC points and 31.3 Final SR points. The improvement is particularly large on Transport, PegInsertionSide, and StackCube. Can is the exception in NAUC: its pretrained success rate is already 87.4%, and LP-DS reaches 93.0 NAUC compared with 91.6 for CNDO and 91.4 for LPS. The LPS port obtains 41.7 average NAUC and 50.2% average Final SR. The results indicate that spherical latent geometry is not an effective constraint across these five tasks. CNDO closes the sample-efficiency gap to the action-space reference. The average NAUC of CNDO is 69.5, compared with 68.5 for DICE. But DICE has the higher average Final SR (87.3% versus 81.5%), mostly because it reaches 94.1% on StackCube. Thus, CNDO is the strongest noise-space method and brings noise-space learning to an overall sample-efficiency level comparable to DICE. 

C. Ablation Studies of Soft Conservative Constraint 

This experiment addresses Q2 . We compare full CNDO with different components in Fig. 5 that set the conservative critic weight to zero or force the execution gate to one. We additionally include LP-DS as a geometrically constrained directional-optimization reference; unlike CNDO, LP-DS enforces a fixed latent bound. Removing conservative critic lowers average NAUC from 69.5 to 58.2. It prevents a severe failure on PegInsertionSide, where Final SR falls from 83.1% to 0.1% without it. This supports using the conservative critic to improve stability when the fixed latent feasible region is removed. Always executing the edit lowers average NAUC to 57.5 and Final SR to 72.2%. The largest NAUC losses occur on Transport (71.2 to 46.4), PegInsertionSide (65.8 to 49.7), and StackCube (50.9 to 36.6). The gate primarily improves sample efficiency and cross-task robustness, rather than guaranteeing a higher endpoint on every task. Finally, full CNDO exceeds the geometrically constrained LP-DS comparator by 9.6 average NAUC points, consistent with the hypothesis that a fixed latent region can reject useful decoded improvements.

E. Real-world Experiments 

We validate DSRL, DICE, CNDO, and CANDO on four tasks using the Simplexity Robotics i7PRO dual-arm robot shown in Fig. 7, equipped with two Wuji hands, one Gemini 335Lg head camera, and two Gemini 305g wrist cameras. All tasks are performed at fixed positions without position randomization. After each policy rollout an episode, we perform 50 residual reinforcement learning update steps. We roll out 20 episodes for each policy and task. The online training process is provided in the supplementary video and on our project website. As shown in Fig. 8, CANDO achieves the highest average success rate of 87.5%, followed closely by CNDO at 86.25%, while DSRL, DICE, and the frozen base policy achieve 55.0%, 42.5%, and 40.0%, respectively. The success rate of each task in Fig. 8 is measured over 20 independent evaluation trials after residual RL training. The online adaptation process shows that CANDO improves rapidly from the early stage and CNDO continues to improve steadily, whereas DICE exhibits a less consistent trend over prolonged training. Overall, the real-world results resemble the pattern observed on PegInser-tionSide: CNDO consistently outperforms DSRL and DICE, while CANDO provides only a small additional gain over CNDO. This result is consistent with our simulation findings: once the noise-space learner itself becomes sufficiently strong, joint training tends to achieve performance comparable to the stronger single-space method, rather than consistently surpassing the best single-space method on every task. V. C ONCLUSION 

This work introduced Conservative Noise Directional Optimization (CNDO) and Conservative Action-Noise Direc-tional Optimization (CANDO) for sample-efficient residual RL. CNDO improves noise-space residual learning without imposing a fixed latent feasible region. It optimizes Gaussian-anchored residual directions with conservative value estima-tion and uses a target-critic supervised gate to selectively execute trusted decoded edits. Across simulation and real-world experiments, CNDO substantially outperforms existing noise-space methods and achieves overall sample efficiency comparable to the strong action-space baseline DICE. Build-ing on this stronger noise-space learner, CANDO jointly optimizes pre-decoding noise directions and post-decoding action residuals at the complete edited action which achieves the highest average sample efficiency. We also figure out that access to both adaptation spaces is not sufficient by itself: joint residual training is most beneficial when the standalone noise-space and action-space learners have comparable capability.
