## Abstract 

We study cooperative multi-agent reinforce-ment learning with decoupled dynamics and a monotone submodular reward function over joint trajectories. Due to the non-additive reward, an optimal policy may depend on the history of all agents. This dependence makes exact optimization exponential in the horizon and the number of agents. To overcome this challenge while retaining theoretical guaran-tees, we introduce pre-window curvature cm,which quantifies how states visited more than 

m steps earlier reduce current marginal gains. Using this curvature to control the error from restricting the policy of each agent to depend only on its last m + 1 states, we propose a greedy algorithm that exploits the decoupled dynamics to optimize local policies sequen-tially. For fixed m, the algorithm achieves a 1−cm 

> 2−cm

approximation guarantee up to es-timation error, and its computation time is polynomial in the number of agents and the horizon. We recover the known 1 /2-factor approximation for time-additive rewards and obtain an approximation factor no smaller than the known total-curvature bound for a single agent. We formulate multi-agent traffic monitoring as an example of this framework and evaluate our algorithm on it. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

In standard reinforcement learning (RL), a single agent maximizes an expected sum of stage rewards, which depend on the agent’s current state and action. Ex-tending the single-agent formulation, cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) studies a team of agents that coordinate their actions to maximize a 

Emails: {yasaman.zolfimoselo, nikolas.geroliminis, maryam.kamgarpour}@epfl.ch 

common objective. Each stage reward then depends on the agents’ current joint state and action. A further simplifying assumption is that each stage reward is a sum of the agents’ local rewards [19]. These two forms of additivity separate rewards over time and across agents, respectively. However, in many cooperative applications, the objective is not additive over time or across agents. Without these forms of additivity, an optimal policy may depend on the history of all agents. In this paper, we consider a common monotone submod-ular reward defined over the visits along the trajectories of all agents. A visit is a time-state pair ( t, s ) indicating that at least one agent is in state s at time t. Submod-ularity captures diminishing returns both across agents and over time. This setting is relevant to informative sensing and path planning [10, 22], cooperative cover-age and biodiversity monitoring [16], and multi-agent persistent monitoring [2, 5, 20]. Traffic monitoring with multiple unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) [9, 12, 15] is a form of persistent monitoring that we use as a running example. In this example, monitoring a road adds less value if it was monitored recently or if another UAV monitors it at the same time. To study such re-wards, we formulate a submodular multi-agent Markov decision process (MDP) with decoupled dynamics, in which the next state of each agent depends only on its own current state and action. 

1.1 Related Work Submodular maximization and path plan-ning. The greedy algorithm achieves a (1 − 1/e )-approximation for maximizing a monotone submodular set function under a cardinality constraint [14]. This guarantee improves when the function has total curva-ture c < 1 [6]. These results were extended to settings where the objective function depends on the order of selection [13, 23, 25]. In all these works, removing ele-ments from a feasible selection leaves it feasible. In our setting, visits must form trajectories consistent with the dynamics, so removing a visit can make a feasible selec-tion infeasible. Informative path planning maximizes 1arXiv:2611.04774v1 [cs.LG] 3 Nov 2026 Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning with Submodular Trajectory Rewards 

submodular objectives under such constraints [3, 21]. For multiple robots, sequential allocation yields a team guarantee when each robot’s marginal problem under fixed predecessor policies is solved optimally or with an approximation guarantee [1, 21, 22]. These planning models assume deterministic transition dynamics. Un-der stochastic dynamics, agents must optimize policies that account for uncertain next states. 

Submodular RL. Submodular RL extends these planning models to stochastic dynamics. In this setting, an agent maximizes an expected monotone submodular reward over visits along its trajectory [17, 24]. Global RL generalizes submodular RL to broader classes of trajectory rewards and proposes semi-gradient meth-ods for their optimization [7]. For submodular re-wards, constant-factor approximation is generally in-tractable [17], but a (1 − c)-factor approximation is possible with total curvature c < 1 [7, 17]. These re-sults are limited to a single agent. Treating a team as one agent makes the joint state space exponential in the number of agents. Moreover, diminishing returns across agents at the same time step can make the (1 −c)guarantee vacuous even for time-additive rewards, as we show in Section 3.1. 

Submodular MARL. In the multi-agent setting, Chen et al. [4] consider time-additive rewards that are monotone submodular across agents at each stage. Under known decoupled dynamics, they optimize the policies sequentially and obtain a 12 -approximation in time polynomial in the number of agents. Liu et al. [11] study stage rewards defined as marginal gains of a submodular trajectory reward, but their guarantee requires every choice to remain feasible regardless of earlier actions. Hence, the guarantees of Chen et al. [4] and Liu et al. [11] do not cover rewards with diminishing returns over time when the dynamics constrain future visits. 

Multi-agent persistent monitoring. Persistent monitoring coordinates mobile agents to revisit impor-tant locations [2, 5]. Closest to our setting, Rezazadeh and Kia [20] consider rewards that grow with the time since the last visit and plan over a receding horizon under deterministic dynamics. Their 12 -factor approxi-mation is relative to the optimum within each planning horizon, not to the full-horizon optimal return. To our knowledge, no prior work gives a polynomial-time algorithm with a curvature-dependent guarantee for MARL with submodular trajectory rewards when stochastic dynamics constrain future visits. Our formu-lation addresses this setting under decoupled dynamics and contains the settings of Chen et al. [4] and Prajapat et al. [17] as special cases, as Remark 1 shows. 

1.2 Contributions 

Building on the above past work, our contributions are summarized as follows. 1. We introduce a new notion of curvature called pre-window curvature cm, which measures the maxi-mum relative reduction in marginal gains caused by visits before an m-step window. 2. Using this notion of curvature, we modify the objective so that stage rewards depend only on the visits of all agents in the last m + 1 steps. Jointly maximizing this objective yields a policy that achieves a (1 − cm)-approximation of the op-timal return. 3. We develop Greedy Windowed Policy Optimiza-tion (GWPO), which sequentially optimizes each agent’s policy using marginal rewards based on complete predecessor trajectories and its own m-step history. For fixed m, it runs in polynomial time and achieves the 1−cm 

> 2−cm

approximation guar-antee of Theorem 2 relative to the optimal global history-dependent return. 4. We formulate multi-UAV traffic monitoring as a submodular multi-agent MDP, capturing diminish-ing returns from repeated monitoring across UAVs and time, and we evaluate GWPO in this setting.

## 3 HARDNESS AND CURVATURE 

To further motivate our results, we first explain why finding a policy that maximizes J(π) in (3) is compu-tationally hard. This hardness justifies restricting the class of rewards to obtain constant-factor guarantees. We then introduce pre-window curvature cm, a prop-erty of the reward function that our approximation guarantees depend on. 

3.1 Computational Hardness 

Maximizing J poses two computational challenges. First, the single-agent case N = 1 admits no constant-factor approximation in polynomial time under stan-dard complexity assumptions, even with deterministic dynamics [17, Theorem 1]. For stochastic dynamics, 4Yasaman Zolfimoselo, Nikolas Geroliminis, Maryam Kamgarpour 

optimal policies of this case may require history depen-dence, and representing an arbitrary history-dependent policy can require space exponential in the horizon [17]. Second, the joint state and action spaces of N agents have sizes |S| N and |A| N , so optimizing directly over them takes time exponential in N .Constant-factor guarantees therefore require further restricting the class of rewards. For a single agent, existing algorithms achieve a (1 − c)-approximation for submodular trajectory rewards with total curvature c < 

1 [7, 17]. For multiple agents under decoupled dynamics, Chen et al. [4] obtain a 1 /2-approximation for time-additive rewards without any curvature assumption. Our framework contains both settings, so we seek a single curvature condition that yields an approximation guarantee for our problem and recovers or improves the known guarantees when specialized to either setting. Total curvature does not achieve this goal in the multi-agent setting, since simultaneous visits can make c = 1 even for time-additive rewards. ## F EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS 

F.1 Environment and Evaluation 

The traffic-monitoring instance uses a 20 × 20 grid and horizon H = 20. Each UAV moves deterministically to an adjacent node or hovers. An action that leaves the grid keeps the UAV in place. Initial positions in the reported comparisons are sampled independently and uniformly over grid nodes. The road map is generated with seed 17 and fixed across methods and algorithm seeds. Road importance is not uniform across the grid. As in real urban networks, a few congested regions require more frequent monitoring than the rest. We model this pattern with two regions of high-importance roads. For roads outside these regions, the maximum importance we is sampled from N (1 .5, 12) and clipped to [0 , 3.5]. For roads inside these regions, 

we is sampled from N (3 .5, 0.52) and clipped to [2 .5, 5.5]. Road importance recovers according to αℓ = 1 − e−βℓ . In the time-additive case, αℓ = 1 for every positive gap. Returns are divided by P 

> e∈E

we, the sum of the maximum importances of all roads. Repeated visits after recovery can contribute additional reward, so the normalized horizon return can exceed one. 

Policy evaluation. Every policy is evaluated by its return J on 10,000 common initial joint states. During evaluation, SubPO selects the action with the highest probability for each UAV, while GWPO and GPO return deterministic policies. All reported confidence intervals are computed across algorithm seeds using Student’s 

t-distribution at the 95% confidence level, with a fixed map and evaluation states. The memory comparison in Figure 2(a) uses N = 20, m ∈ { 0, 1, 2, 3}, and five algorithm seeds. For each seed, we express the return improvement of GWPO over GPO as a percentage of the GPO return, and then average these percentages across seeds. In Figure 3, each SubPO variant uses five training seeds, while GWPO with m = 3 and GPO each use a single seed. In the figure, each return gap is measured relative to the GWPO return from this single seed. The confidence intervals of the gaps therefore reflect only the variation across SubPO seeds.

For each computation-time budget, we selected the policy with the highest validation return reached within that budget.
