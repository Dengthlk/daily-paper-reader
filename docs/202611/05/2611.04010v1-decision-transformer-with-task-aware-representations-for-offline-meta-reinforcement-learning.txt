# ABSTRACT 

To enable effective generalization in offline meta-RL, it is essential to accurately infer the underlying task through latent task representations, which provide a compact and transferable summary of task-specific characteristics for guiding policy generation. In this paper, we propose Contrastive Task-Aware Decision Transformer (CoTA-DT) , a DT-based offline meta-RL framework that learns task representations by jointly integrating four complementary components: (i) 

contrastive learning from offline trajectories, (ii) alignment with natural-language task descriptions, (iii) one-step supervision via a task-conditioned world model, and (iv) long-term supervision via a task-conditioned Q function. The learned task representations are used as conditioning signals for a DT-based policy, en-abling task-aware meta-policy learning. To further support generalization in both zero-shot and few-shot settings, we design a language-grounded, self-adaptive strategy that enables effective and reliable adaptation to unseen tasks. Notably, in CoTA-DT, the pretrained LLM serves as a shared backbone for language en-coding, world modeling, Q-function estimation, and policy learning. This unified design facilitates parameter sharing, improving both efficiency and generalization. Extensive experiments across diverse offline meta-RL benchmarks demonstrate that CoTA-DT consistently outperforms prior methods, particularly in challenging zero-shot generalization scenarios. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Offline meta-reinforcement learning (RL) aims to learn transferable priors from offline trajectories across related tasks, enabling efficient adaptation to new tasks with little or no additional data. In recent years, a prominent line of work in offline meta-RL has built on Decision Transformers (DTs) (Chen et al., 2021), leveraging the strong generalization capabilities of transformer architec-tures (Mitchell et al., 2021; Lin et al., 2022). These DT-based approaches formulate policy learning as sequence modeling over trajectories and perform adaptation through sequence conditioning. For example, Prompt-DT (Xu et al., 2022) leverages expert demonstrations as prompts to encode task-specific information, enabling prompt-based adaptation at test time. Generalized Decision Trans-formers (GDT) (Furuta et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2023) incorporate hindsight signals, such as reward statistics or retrospective summaries, as conditioning inputs for task adaptation. Meta-DT (Wang et al., 2024) conditions the policy on compact task representations learned from trajectories to support generalization. More recently, T2DA (Zhang et al., 2025) conditions a DT-based policy on text embeddings derived from natural-language task descriptions. Despite the advances, these approaches typically learn task identifiers from a single source—such as language, trajectory data, or reward signals—and use them as conditioning inputs to guide DT-based policies for generalization. When such signals are ambiguous, noisy, or distributionally shifted relative to training, task identification can become unreliable, leading to degraded adaptation performance. These limitations naturally raise 1arXiv:2611.04010v1 [cs.LG] 2 Nov 2026 Preprint a question: How can we learn an offline meta-policy that achieves reliable task identification and supports robust generalization in both few-shot and zero-shot settings? 

Reliable task identification across a broad family of related tasks depends on the availability of task-specific information. In offline meta-RL, collected trajectories serve as the primary source of information and implicitly encode task differences. However, relying solely on offline data is often insufficient, especially when the data are limited, noisy, or provide only partial coverage of the task space. Beyond trajectories, additional sources of information are often available. For example, natural-language task descriptions derived from task-level metadata—such as task goals, names, relevant objects, and environment constraints—provide complementary high-level semantics that are not always recoverable from trajectories alone. Meanwhile, environment transitions and rewards vary across tasks, offering informative one-step signals of task-specific dynamics and objectives, while cumulative returns ( e.g. , Q functions) capture long-horizon goals and provide global signals for distinguishing tasks. These observations motivate jointly leveraging multiple sources of information to learn robust task representations and enable reliable task identification in offline meta-RL. In this paper, we propose Contrastive Task-Aware Decision Transformer (CoTA-DT) , a DT-based offline meta-RL framework that learns task representations by jointly integrating four complementary components: (i) contrastive task encoding from offline trajectories, (ii) alignment with natural-language task descriptions, (iii) one-step supervision via a task-conditioned world model, and (iv) 

long-term supervision via a task-conditioned Q function. Specifically, task representations are first learned from trajectories via a contrastive objective that pulls together representations from the same task while separating those from different tasks. However, trajectory-only representations can be noisy due to data variability. To better capture underlying task objectives and reduce bias, we further align them with natural-language task descriptions encoded by a pretrained large language model (LLM), promoting semantic consistency between trajectory- and language-derived information. To further refine the representations, we introduce a task-conditioned world model that provides one-step supervision through transitions and rewards, along with a task-conditioned Q function that offers long-horizon supervision over task objectives. These complementary signals regularize the representations, and all components are optimized jointly to learn task representations from multiple perspectives. The resulting task representations are used as conditioning signals for a DT-based policy, enabling task-aware meta-policy learning. To support generalization in both zero-shot and few-shot settings, we design a language-grounded, self-adaptive strategy for reliable adaptation. Notably, in CoTA-DT, the pretrained LLM serves as a shared backbone for language encoding, world modeling, Q-function estimation, and policy learning. This unified design enables parameter sharing across stages and leverages the LLM’s prior knowledge from large-scale pretraining, along with its strong sequence modeling and transfer capabilities under prompt conditioning (Brown et al., 2020; Ouyang et al., 2022; Touvron et al., 2023), thereby improving both efficiency and generalization. Our main contributions are as follows: 

• We introduce CoTA-DT, an offline meta-RL framework whose policy conditions on a task latent that supports both language-based initialization and updates from observed transitions. • We learn discriminative, language-grounded task representations that capture task dynamics and long-horizon returns, supporting adaptation to unseen tasks with a shared LoRA-adapted LLM backbone. • We develop zero-shot deployment through language-grounded latent inversion and few-shot adapta-tion through support-based initialization, with optional policy fine-tuning. • Extensive experiments across various offline meta-RL benchmarks demonstrate that CoTA-DT con-sistently outperforms prior methods, particularly in challenging zero-shot generalization settings 1.

# 2 RELATED WORK 

Offline Meta-RL. Offline RL learns policies from fixed datasets without further environment interac-tion (Levine et al., 2020; Konyushkova et al., 2020). Extending this setting, offline meta-RL aims to learn transferable priors that enable generalization across tasks using only offline data (Mitchell et al., 2021; Pong et al., 2021; Dorfman et al., 2021; Li et al., 2020a). A large body of work in

However, applying TD learning in the offline setting is challenging due to the deadly triad (Levine et al., 2020), which can lead to instability and error accumulation. To mitigate this issue, many approaches impose constraints to keep learned policies within the support of the dataset (Ajay et al., 2022; Kumar et al., 2020; Kostrikov et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2020b; Kidambi et al., 2020). Other works pursue more stable alternatives, such as return-conditioned or sequence modeling approaches based on Decision Transformers (Wang et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025). 

LLMs for RL. Recent work explores leveraging the representation and generalization capabilities of LLMs for RL, including using them as policy backbones or for reward modeling (Morad et al., 2024; Reed et al., 2022; Brohan et al., 2023). In the offline RL setting, LLMs are incorporated in several ways. LaMo (Shi et al., 2023) demonstrates that pretrained language models can be adapted as effective policy backbones, while KALM (Pang et al., 2024) leverages LLM-generated rollouts to augment offline datasets. More recently, T2DA (Zhang et al., 2025) conditions decision models on text embeddings derived from LLMs to support generalization in offline meta-RL, and LLMDPD (Zhang & Guo, 2025) further enhances generalization by integrating LLM priors into diffusion-based policy learning. In contrast, our method leverages LLMs as a shared backbone for both task representation learning and policy modeling.

5.3 ABLATION STUDIES 

We ablate each component of CoTA-DT across all four benchmarks (Table 4). The findings are summarized as follows: (i) Removing any representation-learning loss reduces performance across all four benchmarks. # 6 CONCLUSION 

We present CoTA-DT, an offline meta-RL framework that learns task representations by integrating contrastive discrimination, language alignment, world-modeling, and Q learning within a LoRA adapter on an LLM backbone. At adaptation, this shared backbone enables zero-shot self-adaptive rollout by inferring an initial representation aligned with the task description, and few-shot adaptation by combining data- and language-grounded initialization with lightweight fine-tuning. Across four benchmarks, CoTA-DT consistently outperforms baselines in both zero-shot and few-shot settings. REPRODUCIBILITY STATEMENT

# A EXTENDED RELATED WORK 

We provide a more detailed account of the four lines of work most relevant to CoTA-DT. 

TD-based offline meta-RL. A long line of work tackles offline meta-RL via temporal-difference learning combined with task inference. PEARL (Rakelly et al., 2019) introduced probabilistic context inference using a permutation-invariant encoder, sparking a family of context-based methods. FOCAL (Li et al., 2020b) extends this to the offline setting via a contrastive trajectory encoder, while subsequent work addresses train/test context distribution shift (Lin et al., 2022; Yuan & Lu, 2022; Gao et al., 2023). UNICORN (Li et al., 2024) provides a unifying information-theoretic view, framing all of these objectives as different upper bounds of mutual information between the latent and task-relevant trajectory statistics. While effective when behaviour policies are diverse, these methods inherit the deadly-triad instability of offline TD learning and seldom exploit external semantic signals such as language. 

DT-based offline meta-RL. Decision Transformer (Chen et al., 2021) reframes RL as return-conditioned sequence modelling, sidestepping bootstrapping and Bellman backups. Prompt-DT (Xu et al., 2022) prepends expert trajectory snippets as a prompt. Generalised DT (Furuta et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2023) replaces this prompt with hindsight statistics. Meta-DT (Wang et al., 2024) learns a disentangled context encoder via a world model and feeds its latent into a DT. Algorithm Distillation (Laskin et al., 2023) distils RL training trajectories into a causal transformer for in-context improvement. HarmoDT (Hu et al., 2024) mitigates parameter-level conflicts in multi-task DTs. With the exception of T2DA, these methods operate purely on numeric trajectories and require non-trivial test-time context to identify the task. 

Language-conditioned policies. BC-Z (Jang et al., 2022) demonstrated that a frozen sentence encoder is sufficient for zero-shot manipulation if many language-annotated demonstrations are available. BAKU (Haldar et al., 2024) extends this with modular per-task action heads on a shared trunk. Both methods rely entirely on the language signal and ignore any decision-side representation learning. T2DA-T (Zhang et al., 2025) bridges the two regimes by aligning text embeddings to dynamics-aware decision embeddings via CLIP-style contrastive learning, then conditioning a DT on the aligned text vector. CoTA-DT extends this idea in three directions: (i) we jointly (rather than separately) optimise the alignment and grounding objectives; (ii) we add long-horizon Q supervision in addition to the one-step world-model losses used in T2DA; and (iii) we share a single LoRA adapter across all four signals and the policy, yielding a unified backbone instead of per-component encoders. 

LLMs as backbones for sequential decision making. Pretrained LLMs have been used as policy initialisations (Shi et al., 2023), conflict-resolution (Wang et al., 2025), and data augmentation (Pang et al., 2024) engines for offline RL. Most existing approaches treat the LLM as a single-purpose component. CoTA-DT instead reuses one frozen LLM with a single LoRA adapter as a shared backbone for language encoding, world modelling, value estimation, and policy generation, yielding a ∼10 × parameter-efficiency advantage over running independent encoders. 

# B BASELINE DESCRIPTIONS 

We compare CoTA-DT to six representative baselines covering language-conditioned, prompt-based, and context-based paradigms. 

Prompt-DT (Xu et al., 2022). Extends DT to meta-RL by prepending expert trajectory snippets to the return-state-action sequence; in-context learning provides task adaptation. Its main limitation is the dependence on test-time expert prompts. 

BC-Z (Jang et al., 2022). Language-conditioned imitation that parameterises the policy as π(a |

s, e lang ), where elang is a sentence embedding from a frozen language encoder. It uses only the text channel and ignores decision-side representation learning. 14 Preprint 

BAKU (Haldar et al., 2024). Multi-task BC transformer with a shared trunk and modular per-task action heads. We use the language-conditioned variant: the BC-Z-style description embedding replaces the per-task identifier, allowing zero-shot evaluation. 

Meta-DT (Wang et al., 2024). Context-based offline meta-RL that disentangles a world-model-trained context encoder from a downstream DT policy. The latent is fed to the DT as a prefix token. Meta-DT does not exploit any natural-language signal. 

UNICORN (Li et al., 2024). A unified information-theoretic framework for context-based offline meta-RL, formulating task identification as MI maximisation between the latent and task-relevant trajectory statistics, while regularising against data-collection signatures. UNICORN generalises FOCAL- and CSRO-style contrastive encoders. 

T2DA-T (Zhang et al., 2025). The strongest published language-conditioned baseline. T2DA-T pre-trains a dynamics-aware trajectory encoder via a world-model objective, aligns text embeddings to those decision embeddings via CLIP-style contrastive learning, and conditions a DT on the aligned text vector. CoTA-DT differs in three ways: (i) world-model and contrastive alignment are jointly optimised instead of staged; (ii) long-horizon Q supervision is added; (iii) a single LoRA adapter is shared across all four pre-training signals and the policy.

# D IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS 

Backbone and adapters. We use the open-source LLaMA-3.2-1B (Touvron et al., 2023); the base 1B parameters θbase are always frozen. A single LoRA adapter θL (rank r=8 , α=16 , applied to all q_proj and v_proj ) is shared across the four prediction heads ( frew , f dyn , f Q, f act ) and across both training phases. Adapter size is ≈ 0.85 M parameters, giving the framework a ∼10 ×

parameter-efficiency advantage over running independent transformers per signal. 

Context encoder. Eϕ is a 1-layer GRU (hidden 256 , output dz =64 ) followed by linear L2-normalisation, ≈ 0.36 M parameters. Sub-trajectory horizon H=20 across all environments. 

Continuous-token projectors. gz , g s, g a, g R are linear projections R• → R2048 matching LLaMA’s hidden size. The text branch reuses the LLaMA token embedding. 

CLIP-style alignment. Proj z , Proj h are 2-layer MLPs R{64 ,2048 } → R128 followed by L2-normalisation. The temperature κβ is parameterised as κβ = exp(log κβ ) with log κβ initialised at 

log(0 .07) and clamped to [0 .01 , 1.0] . Three design choices are critical: (i) keeping CLIP gradients unblocked on both sides (without this the loss stays at ln |I| ), (ii) the learnable temperature, and (iii) 

a 10-epoch CLIP warm-up. 

Representation learning phase. We optimise Eq. (7) with (λ1, λ 2, λ 3, λ 4) = (0 .1, 0.5, 1.0, 0.1) 

and α = 1 .0, where λ1–λ4 correspond to the weights on LNCE , Lalign , (Lrew +αLdyn ) and LQ

respectively. Adam, lr 3×10 −4, batch 128 , 200 epochs (Point-Robot 80 ). We found this scale-invariant target essential, since unnormalised returns are dominated by tasks with large reward magnitudes. 

Policy learning phase. We initialise from the representation-learning checkpoint, replace the auxiliary heads with fact , and continue to fine-tune θL together with fact via Lpolicy (Eq. 8). DT input format is [z, ˆR1, s 1, a 1, ˆR2, s 2, a 2, . . . ] with z as a prefix token, refreshed by re-encoding the most recent H steps. AdamW, lr 5×10 −5, weight decay 10 −4, batch 64 (PR) / 32 (CV, AD, MW), DT horizon 20 (PR) / 40 otherwise; 10 5 training steps with 5,000 -step linear warm-up; gradient clipping 0.25 .

Zero-shot inversion. Eq. (9) is solved with Adam, 200 steps, lr 0.01 , on a fresh z initialised at 0.Empirically convergence happens within ∼ 50 steps; the remainder tightens the latent norm. 

Few-shot fine-tuning. Given k support trajectories, we obtain z0 via data-grounded initialisation (starting from ¯z) followed by the same CLIP-space inversion as zero-shot (Eq. 9); we then hold z0

fixed and fine-tune only θL and fact for 100 steps with Adam, lr 5×10 −5. The full adaptation costs 

< 1 minute on a single A6000. 

Compute. All experiments run on multi-GPU servers equipped with NVIDIA RTX A6000 (48 GB) GPUs; each training job uses a single GPU and we run 4 jobs in parallel on a 4 ×A6000 node. The representation phase takes ∼3 h on Point-Robot and ∼6 h on Meta-World; the policy phase takes 

∼2-3 h. A single forward pass (one trajectory of length H=20 , batch 128 ) takes ∼18 ms, dominated by the LLM backbone. 

Note on supplementary code. For historical reasons, our supplementary code uses an [st; zt]

concatenation and per-step sliding-window updates instead of the prefix-token + per-window scheme described in Sec. 4.1. The paper version is ∼5% better on Ant-Dir and matches elsewhere; the code variant is preserved as the concat_z ablation. Numbers reported in Tables 1–4 use the paper version. # G VISUALISATION ANALYSIS 

Beyond the qualitative t-SNE visualisation in Fig. 3 of the main paper, we quantify the alignment by computing the rank-1 retrieval accuracy of the cross-modal nearest neighbour ( ¯zi ↔ wi) on Point-Robot. The accuracy reaches 90 % on Expert , 88 % on Medium , and drops to 82% on the het-erogeneous Mixed dataset. We attribute the Mixed-dataset drop to the bimodal trajectory distribution (medium ∪ expert) producing a less concentrated ¯zi, which is itself an empirical finding about how dataset heterogeneity affects representation tightness. The same trend—highest retrieval accuracy on Expert, lowest on Mixed—also holds on Meta-World ( 85% / 81% / 78% ), confirming the observation is not Point-Robot-specific.
