# H2MARL: Hierarchical Heterogeneous Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Robot Control 

Xuemin Hu 1, Kaixin Li 1, Qingqing Xia 1, Yuxin Wang 1, Wei Xie 1, Bo Tang 2, and Long Chen 3

Abstract — Coordinated whole-body control of humanoid robots remains challenging due to high-dimensional coupled dynam-ics and substantial physical heterogeneity across joint degrees of freedom (JDoFs). Existing multi-agent reinforcement learning methods either employ coarse agent partitions or directly apply generic heterogeneous-agent algorithms, while hierarchical con-trollers commonly optimize high-level planning and low-level ex-ecution independently, leading to uncoordinated motion. In this paper, we propose H 2MARL, a hierarchical heterogeneous multi-agent reinforcement learning framework with two key mechanisms. We first formulate humanoid whole-body control using a JDoF-level physical-heterogeneity-aware policy factorization, where each torque-actuated JDoF is conditioned on an explicit descriptor of its actuation, kinematic, and dynamic properties. Moreover, we develop a closed-loop policy–executor co-optimization mechanism that jointly adapts the high-level pose planner and the low-level joint executor using shared interaction trajectories, thereby reducing the distribution mismatch between planning and execution. Extensive experiments in MuJoCo and Isaac Gym demonstrate that H 2MARL consistently improves locomotion stability, gait coordination, and motion quality over homogeneous, coarse-grained, and indepen-dently optimized alternatives. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Coordinated whole-body control (WBC) is a major chal-lenge in achieving highly human-like movement for hu-manoid robot (HR) research [1]. The current mainstream methods are based on imitation learning and have achieved remarkable results [2], [3]. However, these methods require a large amount of labeled data through motion capture systems or teleoperation, resulting in high training costs. Moreover, some marginal data, such as actions in complex and dan-gerous industrial and outer space operations, are difficult to collect, limiting the application of these methods [4]. Reinforcement learning (RL)-based methods [5] mainly in-volve single-agent RL methods, which can complete motions such as walking and running in simulators without a large amount of data and exhibit good coordination capabilities for HRs with low degrees of freedom (DoF) [6], [7], but these methods struggle to achieve precise coordination across joints with numerous joints and high DoF.

To address these issues, some researchers introduce multi-agent reinforcement (MARL), where joints or limbs are treated as multiple homogeneous agents [8], [9], as shown in Fig. 1(a). However, in fact, various joints in the human body exhibit obvious differences in muscle composition, move-ment directions, dimensions, and coupling [10]. For HRs, their hip joints (high torque and heavy load) and elbow joints (high sensitivity and light load) also exhibit fundamental physical heterogeneity in dynamic characteristics, DoF, and roles in whole-body balance [10]. Existing homogeneous MARL-based methods overlook heterogeneous attributes in joint and limbs and fail to learn effective actions for different agents with the same objectives but different decisions, generating incongruous motions. Regarding the structure of the control model, some re-searchers adopt the end-to-end framework that directly maps observations and proprioceptions to control actions [10], [2], [11]. These methods are simple and effective for humanoid robots with low DoF, but struggle to train an effective model in state-action spaces for HRs with high DoF. Other researchers employ a hierarchical model [12] that separates high-level task planning from low-level joint control, where the high-level model often uses a large model combined imitation learning and the low-level model typically employs terminal controllers to perform planning. Nevertheless, these methods often decouple the learning of the high-level plan-ning model and the low-level control models, often leading to insufficient coordination between high- and low-level models and stiff motions. In addition, the large model used in the high-level model often leads to a long inference time and poor real-time performance. arXiv:2611.06523v1 [cs.RO] 4 Nov 2026 To address these issues, we propose a hierarchical het-erogeneous multi-agent reinforcement learning (H 2MARL) for coordinating humanoid whole-body control, which offers the following three contributions: 1) We propose a JDoF-level physical-heterogeneity-aware policy factorization. The humanoid whole-body control is formulated as a cooperative heterogeneous multi-agent problem at the joint-degree-of-freedom (JDoF) level, where each torque-actuated JDoF is conditioned on an explicit physical descriptor containing its actuation, kinematic, and dynamic properties, as shown in Fig. 1(b), providing a fine-grained inductive bias that enables different JDoFs to learn specialized control behaviors while maintaining coordination through centralized training. 2) We develop a hierarchical policy–executor coupling mechanism in which the JDoF-level pose planner and joint executor interact with the environment through the same closed-loop trajectory and are iteratively optimized rather than trained independently, preventing the distribution mismatch caused by freezing the executor. 3) Extensive experiments conducted in the MuJoCo and Isaac Gym simulator demonstrate that H2MARL outperforms state-of-the-art methods in metrics for measuring coordination and human-like qualities. Through controlled comparisons of agent granularity, heterogeneous conditioning, and closed-loop collaborative optimization, we also isolate the contribution of each design and demonstrate that performance improvement comes from the proposed JDoF-level policy factorization and policy–executor coupling rather than from directly combining existing HAPPO, HST, and conventional PD components. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Whole-body Control for Humanoid Robots 

Current mainstream methods of humanoid robot WBC are based on large model and imitation learning [13], which can allow robots to achieve human-like locomotion and application in the fields of commercial services [1], [14], home care [1], entertainment performances [11], [2], etc., but they heavily rely on a large number of expert demonstrations, limiting the applications in some hazardous industrial scenar-ios [1]. RL-based methods are increasingly being adopted by researchers due to the advantage of not requiring labeled data in recent years, and have demonstrated robust whole-body locomotion and loco-manipulation on humanoid and legged robots [15], [16], [17]. In particular, hierarchical RL is applied in robot control, where the controller is often divided into two layers, including a high-level planning model that generates abstract locomotion commands or selected skills and a low-level control model that executes specific actions, to enhance learning efficiency and the ability to solve long-range tasks [18]. However, these methods often decouple the high-level planning model and the low-level control model, and the low-level model typically employs homogeneous coarse-grained skill executors, making it difficult to express the fine-grained heterogeneity and coupled coordination among joints in high-DoF HRs, resulting in insufficient co-ordination between high- and low-level models. Instead, we formulate the high-level controller as a set of heterogeneous agents and design a hierarchical policy–executor coupling mechanism to address the issues of homogeneity between joints and inconsistency between the two-level controllers, respectively. 

B. Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Systems 

Heterogeneous multi-agent systems (HMAS) have been applied in the fields of smart logistics, smart grids, and post-disaster search and rescue due to the ability to han-dle complex collaborative tasks [19]. Researchers proposed some classic HMASs such as ROMA [20], RODE [21], and MPN [22] to improve the collaboration performance of agents. However, these methods still follow the paradigm of sharing homogeneous parameters, which is similar to that in MAPPO [23] and IPPO, and only work well in tasks with isomorphic agents. Heterogeneous agent reinforcement learning (HARL) methods such as Heterogeneous-Agent PPO (HAPPO) [24] and HATD3 [9] are proposed for effec-tive collaboration among heterogeneous multi-agents. Some researchers use HARL to control various types of single-robot agents, such as quadrupedal robots and humanoid robots [19], performing high-dimensional actions, alleviat-ing the difficulty of single-agent RL methods in precisely coordinating the motions. However, humanoid robots are not simple combinations of stacked limbs, and different parts of the body have different properties. These methods of unified formulation joints or limbs struggle to represent such fine-grained heterogeneous coupling characteristics at the joint level, leading to insufficient coordination. The most similar method to ours is HAPPO which solves heterogeneous-agent policy optimization, but does not specify how a humanoid’s physically heterogeneous JDoFs should be factorized into agents nor how such policies should be coupled with a temporal executor. Instead, each torque-actuated DoF of joints is formulated as an independent het-erogeneous agent according to the physically heterogeneous characteristics in our method, and the JDoF-level pose plan-ner and joint executor interact with the environment through the same closed-loop trajectory, enhancing the coordination and human-like qualities. POLICY FACTORIZATION 

Humanoid whole-body control is challenged by high-DoF coupling and joint-level physical heterogeneity, where joints differ in torque limits, inertia, degrees of freedom, and kine-matic locations. The WBC methods based on homogeneous parameter sharing impose strong inductive bias on these heterogeneous control units, making it difficult to jointly achieve stability and agility and resulting in incongruous motions. Existing methods typically model the robot as a monolithic controller or adopt coarse limb-level agents. How-ever, heterogeneity also exists within the degrees of freedom of a single joint. Although sharing the same physical body parts, these degrees of freedom differ in rotation axis, torque generation, roles in balance and locomotion, and coupling with adjacent joints. For example, sagittal hip motion mainly contributes to forward propulsion, while lateral hip motion focuses on balance and posture compensation. Formulating them as a single agent compresses multiple control roles into one policy, which is contrary to the structure composition of human bodies and increases optimization difficulty. Motivated by the intrinsic heterogeneity of humanoid actuation dimensions, we propose a JDoF- level physical-heterogeneity-aware policy factorization, where each torque-actuated JDoF is formulated as an independent heteroge-neous agent with physical attributes and functional roles, as shown in Fig. 1. IV. H IERARCHICAL POLICY –EXECUTOR COUPLING MECHANISM 

The proposed hierarchical policy–executor coupling mech-anism consists of a high-level pose planner, a low-level joint executor, and a Closed-loop Policy–Executor Co-Optimization (CPECO) mechanism, as shown in Fig. 2. At the high-level pose planner, each heterogeneous JDoF-level agent takes observation oHi,t as input and outputs a pose increment ∆qHt . The target pose is obtained by adding 

∆qHt to the target pose at the previous time step qtar t−1.The low-level joint executor receives qtar t and historical proprioception Ht and outputs joint positions qposi t , which are converted to joint torques τt by a Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller. Both the high-level policy and the low-level executor are optimized by the coupling trajectories in a closed-loop. 

A. High-Level Pose Planner 

The high-level controller in existing hierarchical WBC methods typically generates absolute target poses of joints, which may be infeasible to track due to dynamic physi-cal constraints and limited control bandwidth of the robot since the performance of the low-level joint executor is not considered in these methods, resulting in unstable joint position tracking and discontinuous target actions. To address this issue, we introduce a pose increment representation method to calculate joint target poses. Instead of predicting absolute poses, the high-level controller is formulated as a heterogeneous pose increment planner and generates adaptive joint target corrections with respect to the current target pose, improving joint action continuity while preserving task-driven adaptability. B. Low-Level Joint Executor 

The high-level controller generates task-oriented target poses, but directly executing these commands is difficult due to the strong temporal dependency and dynamic constraints of humanoid motion. Therefore, a low-level controller is required to transform high-level motion intentions into phys-ically feasible joint commands. Unlike conventional position tracking methods that only consider the current target pose, humanoid motion execution requires historical states to capture motion evolution and contact dynamics to maintain the continuity of the motion. In this paper, we design a low-level joint executor based on the Human State Transformer (HST) [3] as the temporal joint executor due to its ability to represent long-range temporal dependencies and generate context-aware joint trajectories. Single-agent formulation also performs substantially worse than JDoF-level formulation, further demonstrating the impor-tance of explicitly modeling JDoF-level heterogeneity. 

3) Evaluation of different rewards: The slip reward and the alternation reward in Eq. 12 are also the differences between our method and the baselines. To analyze the effect of the two reward terms, we compare the complete reward function with the three variants by separately removing each term and removing both. The results are shown in Table IV. The complete reward function (Complete) achieves the best performance. Compared to the complete reward, removing the slip reward ( w/o Slip ) increases VE, CoT, MJ, CAM, and TS by 100.4%, 595.9%, 972.5%, 550.8%, and 87.9%, respectively, indicating a larger velocity error, higher energy consumption, greater body oscillations, and worse motion stability.Removing the alternation reward ( w/o Alt ) also in-creases VE, CoT, MJ, CAM, and TS by 66.0%, 503.4%, 974.1%, 422.0%, and 25.7%, respectively, indicating that encouraging coordinated left–right stepping substantially improves locomotion accuracy, control efficiency, motion smoothness, and body stability. The method removing both reward terms performs the worst of all.

We proposed H2MARL, a hierarchical heterogeneous multi-agent reinforcement learning method for humanoid whole-body control in this paper. Each torque-actuated JDoF in the robot is formulated as an independent heterogeneous agent to allow the policy to understand the differences in physical properties and functional roles among joints.
