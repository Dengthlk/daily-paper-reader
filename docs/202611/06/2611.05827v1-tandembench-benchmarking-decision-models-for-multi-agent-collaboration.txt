Abstract 

Decision  models  choose  among  predefined  op-

tions  in  tasks  such  as  routing  and  action  se-

lection.  In  multi-agent  collaboration,  a deci-

sion  determines  how  members  divide  work,  ex-

change  resources,  and  act  in  parallel.  We  in-

troduce  TandemBench , a benchmark  of  1,200 

questions  in  multi-agent  cooking  scenarios 

where  coordination  choices  affect  goal  com-

pletion.  Models  receive  complete  global  in-

formation  and  candidate  team  plans,  with  ref-

erence  answers  established  through  execution 

and  exact  search.  Models  select  an  optimal 

team  plan  and,  in  paired  variants  that  retain  or 

remove  it,  judge  whether  better  arrangements 

exist  beyond  the  supplied  options.  We  evaluate 

40  native  decision  models  and  compare  them 

with  10  general-purpose  language  models.  The 

best  native  decision  model  achieves  62.50%  de-

cision  accuracy.  Native  decision  models  re-

main  close  to  chance  in  optimality  verification. 

Paired  tests  reveal  that  some  report  better  alter-

natives  regardless  of  whether  an  optimal  plan 

is  already  present.  These  findings  show  that 

team-plan  decision  accuracy  alone  can  con-

ceal  systematic  errors  in  judging  whether  the 

supplied  options  contain  an  optimal  arrange-

ment.  The  dataset  and  evaluation  code  are 

publicly  available  at  https://github.com/ lililibaohang/TandemBench .

Our  contributions  are  as  follows. 

• We  introduce  TandemBench , a 1,200-

question  benchmark  for  evaluating  decision 

models  on  how  multiple  agents  coordinate 

their  actions  toward  a shared  goal,  given 

complete  global  information.

2 Related  Work 

Decision  Models.  Decision-model  evaluation 

covers  both  individual  choices  and  decisions 

within  agent  workflows.  JEVal  and  OpenDeci-

sions  assess  accuracy,  calibration,  and  inference 

efficiency  (Duan  et  al. , 2026 ; Geuter  et  al. , 2026 ),

while  DecisionRobust  and  DecisionPivot  exam-

ine  adversarial  robustness  and  controlled  input 

changes,  respectively  (Lan  et  al. , 2026 ; Zhuang 

et  al. , 2026 ). AgentBench  and  AgentBoard  eval-

uate  sequential  decisions  through  task  success  and 

progress  (Liu  et  al. , 2024 ; Ma  et  al. , 2024 ); Agent-

DecisionBench  spans  isolated  choices,  recorded 

agent  states,  and  online  control  (Zhu  et  al. , 2026 ).

TandemBench  takes  a joint  team  plan  as  the  deci-

sion  unit,  so  one  choice  specifies  actions  for  multi-

ple  members  toward  a shared  goal. 

Multi-Agent  Collaboration.  CoELA  and  RoCo 

study  communication  and  coordinated  execution, 

while  MultiAgentBench  and  PARTNR  evaluate 

collaboration  in  interactive  settings  (Zhang  et  al. ,

2024 ; Mandi  et  al. , 2024 ; Zhu  et  al. , 2025 ; Chang 

et  al. , 2025 ). CoordQA  and  RoCoBench-Text  as-

sess  coordination  choices  and  reasoning  through 

static  questions  (Agashe  et  al. , 2025 ; Mandi  et  al. ,

2024 ). ParaCook  supplies  CP-SAT  optimal  ref-

erences  for  abstract  scheduling  (Zhang  et  al. ,

2026 ), while  CoCoBench  evaluates  coordination 

against  optimal  makespan  under  a skill  abstrac-

tion  (Chen  et  al. , 2026 ). TandemBench  builds 

on  Collab-Overcooked’s  asymmetric  cooking  en-

vironment  (Sun  et  al. , 2025 ). It  evaluates  a single 

model’s  choices  among  executable  team  plans  un-

der  complete  global  information,  using  an  exact  op-

timality  reference  from  the  same  state. 

Planning  and  Verification.  Planning  bench-

marks  assess  plan  generation  and  reasoning 

about  actions,  state  transitions,  and  con-

straints  (Valmeekam  et  al. , 2023 ; Kokel  et  al. ,

2025 ; Xie  et  al. , 2024 ; Zheng  et  al. , 2024 ). Plan-

optimality  verification  asks  whether  a given  plan 

is  optimal  (Lin  et  al. , 2024 ), while  optimality 

certificates  provide  independently  checkable 

evidence  (Dold  et  al. , 2025 ). DecisionPivot  uses 

controlled  contrasts  to  examine  how  decisions 

respond  to  input  changes  (Zhuang  et  al. , 2026 ).

TandemBench  asks  whether  any  supplied  joint 

team  plan  attains  the  state  optimum.  Paired 

variants  retain  or  remove  the  optimal  plan  while 

keeping  the  state  and  goal  fixed,  testing  whether

Decision-Making 

3.1  Task  Definition 

A single  decision  model  receives  a complete  global 

state,  the  environment  rules,  a shared  goal,  and  can-

didate  team  plans.  Each  candidate  specifies  an  ac-

tion  program  for  each  of  two  team  members.  The 

global  state  records  both  members’  configurations, 

task-relevant  resources,  and  ongoing  processes. 

The  two  programs  execute  concurrently  under 

the  environment  rules.  Their  dependencies  and  re-

source  handoffs  determine  when  each  member  can 

proceed  (Figure  1).  A candidate  may  cover  the  full 

task  or  prescribe  a stage  that  ends  before  the  final 

goal.  Appendix  A specifies  the  execution  seman-

tics. 

3.2  Decision  Objectives 

We  evaluate  a team  plan  against  either  an  interme-

diate  milestone  or  the  final  task  goal,  referred  to  as 

short- and  long-horizon  goals.  Completion  cost  is 

measured  in  joint  execution  steps. 

Short-horizon  goals.  We  count  the  steps  un-

til  the  specified  intermediate  milestone  is  first 

reached. 

Long-horizon  goals.  We  count  the  steps  until 

the  final  goal  is  reached.  If  the  prescribed  stage 

ends  before  completion,  the  cost  is  its  executed 

steps  plus  the  minimum  remaining  steps  from  the 

resulting  state. 

Both  goal  settings  apply  to  team-plan  deci-

sions  and  optimality  verification.  Short- and  long-

horizon  questions  from  the  same  state  use  their  re-

spective  execution  protocols  and  may  have  differ-

ent  candidate  programs.  Appendix  A.2  provides 

the  formal  definitions. 

3.3  Optimal  Decisions 

Both  tasks  use  the  state  optimum:  the  minimum 

completion  cost  of  any  legal  joint  execution  from 

the  given  state  under  the  specified  goal  and  action 

domain. 

For  team-plan  decision-making , the  model 

chooses  among  four  candidate  plans.  Exactly  one 

attains  the  state  optimum  and  is  the  correct  answer. 

Plan-optimality  verification  asks  whether  a

given  plan  is  optimal  (Lin  et  al. , 2024 ). We  eval-

uate  whether  the  supplied  set  contains  such  a plan. 

Each  verification  question  includes  three  concrete 

plans  and  a fourth  option  stating  that  a feasible  plan 

can  reach  the  goal  in  fewer  joint  steps  than  all  three. 

This  statement  is  true  exactly  when  none  of  the  sup-

plied  plans  attains  the  state  optimum. 

The  two  tasks  are  scored  separately.  Decision 

accuracy  scores  the  optimal  concrete  choice.  Ver-

ification  accuracy  scores  whether  the  better-plan 

statement  is  correctly  accepted  or  rejected;  any 

valid  concrete  choice  counts  as  rejection.  Section  5

defines  the  metrics,  and  Appendix  A.2  gives  the 

formal  decision  criteria. 

4 TandemBench 

We  construct  TandemBench  from  states  where  co-

ordination  choices  affect  task  completion.  Alterna-

tive  team  plans  make  these  choices  explicit,  and  ex-

ecution  and  exact  search  establish  their  outcomes 

and  the  state  optimum.  Each  verified  base  question 

yields  paired  variants  that  retain  or  remove  the  op-

timal  plan  (Figure  3). 

4.1  Environment  and  Tasks 

We  use  30  recipe  tasks  from  Collab-

Overcooked  (Sun  et  al. , 2025 ), where  a chef 

and  an  assistant  work  in  separate  kitchen  regions 

with  asymmetric  access  to  ingredients,  appli-

ances,  and  counters.  Access  restrictions  make 

some  actions  depend  on  resources  supplied  by 

the  other  member,  while  recipe  dependencies 

constrain  action  order.  Automatic  processing 

allows  preparation  to  overlap  with  cooking 

(Figure  1).  Carrying  limits  and  shared-counter 

availability  constrain  handoff  locations  and  timing. 

Appendix  A gives  the  execution  rules. 

4.2  Scenario  Selection 

We  select  states  where  fixing  a coordination  choice 

changes  the  best  achievable  completion  cost.  Can-

didate  states  come  from  verified  optimal  delivery 

trajectories,  at  events  that  change  available  materi-

als,  processing  status,  or  handoff  opportunities.  Ex-

ecution  prefixes  establish  reachability. 

At  each  state,  we  compare  alternative  orders  of 

two  required  events  performed  by  the  same  mem-

ber  or  alternative  locations  for  a required  hand-

off.  Exact  search  determines  the  minimum  deliv-

ery  cost  subject  to  each  choice,  optimizing  all  re-

maining  actions.  A state  qualifies  when  two  fea-

sible  choices  are  established:  one  attains  the  state 

optimum,  while  the  other  requires  at  least  three  ad-

ditional  joint  steps. 

We  select  200  qualifying  scenarios  across  all

4.3  Instance  Construction 

Each  scenario  supplies  a short-horizon  and  a

long-horizon  base  question.  From  each  verified 

base  question,  we  derive  a pair  of  optimality-

verification  variants. 

4.3.1  Question  Construction 

Candidate  plans  express  different  ways  to  coordi-

nate  the  same  team  toward  the  specified  goal.  They 

vary  in  work  order,  handoff  location,  preparation 

scope,  work  overlap,  or  waiting  conditions.  For  ex-

ample,  changing  the  order  of  ingredient  supply  and 

utensil  preparation  affects  when  processing  can  be-

gin  and  which  work  can  overlap.  Handoff  choices 

also  depend  on  members’  positions  and  counter 

availability.  Appendix  A.3  gives  concrete  candi-

date  descriptions  and  costs. 

Each  base  question  contains  four  plans,  with  at 

least  three  feasible  and  exactly  one  attaining  the 

state  optimum.  Multiple  feasible  alternatives  re-

quire  comparing  arrangements  that  can  complete 

the  goal.  We  aim  to  include  alternatives  with  small 

cost  gaps  when  they  express  distinct  coordination 

choices.  Candidate  programs  may  differ  between 

the  short- and  long-horizon  objectives. 

To  construct  a verification  pair,  we  remove  the 

optimal  plan  in  one  variant  and  a predetermined 

nonoptimal  plan  in  the  other.  Both  add  the  same 

statement  that  a feasible  plan  can  reach  the  goal 

in  fewer  joint  steps  than  all  three  remaining  plans. 

The  state,  goal,  execution  rules,  and  retained  pro-

grams  remain  unchanged.  The  resulting  four-

option  pair  supports  comparison  of  correct  reports 

when  the  optimum  is  missing  and  false  alarms 

when  it  is  present. 

4.3.2  Answer  Annotation 

Candidate  execution  records  the  steps  taken,  goal 

attainment,  and  resulting  state.  Exact  search  es-

tablishes  the  state  optimum  and  any  optimal  con-

tinuation  needed  for  long-horizon  evaluation.  To-

gether,  these  determine  candidate  costs  under  the 

objectives  in  Section  3. Matching  candidate  costs 

to  the  state  optimum  identifies  the  correct  base  an-

swer.  In  the  verification  pair,  the  better-plan  state-

ment  is  false  when  the  optimum  is  retained  and  true 

when  it  is  removed. 

Noncompletion  requires  evidence  under  the  pre-

scribed  program  and  protocol;  unresolved  searches 

do  not  supply  answer  labels.  Appendix  B.2  details 

candidate  validation  and  content  review. 

4.4  Benchmark  Statistics 

TandemBench  contains  1,200  questions  from  200 

scenarios  across  30  tasks:  400  team-plan  decisions 

and  800  optimality-verification  variants.  The  200 

short- and  200  long-horizon  questions  form  200 

pairs  sharing  the  initial  state.  The  variants  form 

400  pairs,  each  containing  one  question  with  the 

optimum  retained  and  one  with  it  removed.  Both 

types  of  pairs  derive  from  the  same  200  scenar-

ios.  The  two  evaluation  dimensions  are  scored  sep-

arately,  and  option  rotations  do  not  count  as  addi-

tional  questions. In  64  scenarios,  the  short- and  long-horizon 

questions  use  identical  candidate  programs  and  re-

tain  the  same  optimal  choice,  supporting  compar-

isons  across  goals  and  execution  protocols.  In  15 

long-horizon  questions,  a feasible  alternative  fin-

ishes  its  prescribed  stage  sooner  than  the  optimal 

candidate  but  has  a higher  total  completion  cost. 

These  cases  support  analysis  of  choices  where 

stage  length  and  final  cost  favor  different  plans. 

Appendix  B.3  reports  structural  groups  for  related 

recipe  variants  and  programs,  with  grouping  rules 

and  full  counts. 

5 Experiments  and  Analysis 

5.1  Experimental  Setup 

TandemBench  uses  English  inputs.  We  evaluate  40 

native  decision  models  and  report  reference  results 

from  10  general-purpose  language  models.  Un-

resolved  answers  from  these  models  count  as  in-

correct  under  the  scoring  policy.  Each  general-

purpose  model  contributes  19,200  outputs  across 

questions,  option  rotations,  and  samples. 

Each  question  is  evaluated  under  four  cyclic  op-

tion  rotations,  so  each  candidate  appears  once  in 

each  position.  Native  decision  models  produce  one 

response  per  rotation,  and  general-purpose  models 

produce  four.  Each  response  is  scored  separately, 

without  voting.  Models  use  their  native  decision 

or  generation  interfaces;  Appendix  C details  rea-

soning  modes  and  sampling  parameters. 

Decision  accuracy  (DA)  measures  selection  of 

the  optimal  concrete  plan.  Verification  accuracy 

(VA)  averages  the  correct-detection  rate  when  the 

optimum  is  removed  and  the  correct-rejection  rate 

when  it  is  retained.  Any  valid  concrete-plan  choice 

counts  as  rejecting  the  better-plan  statement.  In-

valid  or  truncated  responses  count  as  incorrect  for 

both  metrics.  Uniform  option  selection  yields  25% 

DA  and  50%  VA.  Appendix  D gives  the  metric  for-

mulas. 

5.2  Overall  Performance 

Native  decision  models  vary  widely  in  team-plan 

decision  accuracy,  from  15.44%  to  62.50%  (Ta-

ble  1).  Their  verification  accuracy  remains  near 

chance,  ranging  from  45.94%  to  56.28%. 

The  highest-DA  model,  KnowLine,  does  not 

lead  in  verification:  D3-Ultra  achieves  the  highest 

VA  despite  lower  DA.  Thus,  plan-selection  accu-

racy  alone  does  not  capture  how  well  models  judge 

whether  better  arrangements  exist  beyond  the  sup-

plied  options. 

Among  general-purpose  models  (Table  2; Ap-

pendix  D.2 ),  Qwen3.8-27B  leads  with  82.80%  DA 

and  71.88%  VA. 

5.3  Optimality  Verification 

Many  models  report  a better  plan  at  similar  rates 

whether  the  optimal  plan  is  present  or  absent  (Fig-

ure  4(a)).  Tacit-2B  detects  98.38%  of  cases  where 

the  optimum  is  absent,  but  also  raises  false  alarms 

in  97.88%  of  cases  where  it  is  present. 

Paired  responses  show  two  recurring  patterns: 

some  models  report  a better  plan  in  both  variants, 

while  others  select  a concrete  plan  in  both  (Fig-

ure  4(b)).  Both  patterns  produce  a correct  judg-

ment  in  one  condition  and  an  incorrect  judgment 

in  the  other. 

The  better-plan  probabilities  also  discriminate 

weakly  between  the  two  conditions,  with  a median 

AUROC  of  0.537  and  a maximum  of  0.598  across 

native  models  (Appendix  G). 

5.4  Short- and  Long-Horizon  Goals 

We  compare  64  matched  pairs  that  share  the  initial 

state,  candidate  programs,  and  optimal  choice,  but 

differ  in  their  goal  and  execution  rules. 

Across  native  decision  models,  mean  DA  de-

creases  from  49.58%  for  short-horizon  goals  to 

46.75%  for  long-horizon  goals,  although  individ-

ual  models  vary  (Figure  5).  Full  model-level  re-

sults  appear  in  Appendix  E.2 .

Earlier  stage  completion  can  leave  more  work  for 

the  team.  Appendix  E reports  model-level  choices 

on  15  long-horizon  questions  where  an  alternative 

finishes  its  prescribed  stage  sooner  but  requires 

more  joint  steps  to  reach  the  final  goal. 

5.5  Position  Sensitivity 

We  measure  position  sensitivity  as  the  difference 

between  a model’s  highest  and  lowest  accuracy 

across  four  option  rotations.  The  median  range 

across  native  models  is  10.38  percentage  points,  in-

dicating  that  option  order  affects  measured  accu-

racy  (Figure  6(a) ). 

Choice  consistency  measures  how  often  the 

same  underlying  plan  is  selected  across  all  four  ro-

tations  (Figure  6(b) ).  CLM  selects  the  same  plan 

in  92.00%  of  questions  despite  a DA  of  15.44%.

6 Conclusion 

TandemBench  evaluates  decision  models  on  team 

plans  involving  task  dependencies,  resource  hand-

offs,  and  parallel  work.  Execution  and  exact  search 

establish  reference  answers,  while  paired  candi-

date  sets  test  whether  models  recognize  when  bet-

ter  arrangements  exist  beyond  the  supplied  op-

tions.  Native  decision  models  differ  widely  in  plan-

choice  accuracy,  but  their  reports  of  better  alterna-

tives  often  fail  to  distinguish  whether  an  optimal 

plan  is  present.  Performance  also  varies  across 

goal  settings  and  option  orders.  TandemBench 

therefore  supports  comparisons  that  assess  both  the 

selected  team  plan  and  judgments  about  the  avail-

able  alternatives. 

Limitations 

Our  findings  concern  team-plan  decisions  for  two 

agents  in  one  kitchen  with  global  information.

A Execution  and  Decision  Criteria 

A.1  Execution  Rules 

Each  joint  step  resolves  chef  interaction,  assistant 

interaction,  movement,  then  appliance  processing. 

Processing  includes  its  start  step;  finished  products 

await  retrieval.  Waits  are  checked  at  step  bound-

aries.  An  assistant  interaction  can  retrieve  an  item 

placed  by  the  chef  in  the  same  step;  an  explicit  wait 

cannot. 

Domains  define  role  permissions,  retrieval,  and 

counter  access.  The  canonical  processing-chain 

domain  permits  retrieval  before  processing  starts 

and  plated-product  transfers.  Search  records  re-

quire  matching  states,  goals,  domains,  and  execu-

tion  rules.

B.2  Coverage  and  Candidate  Verification 

For  representative  states  Si and  optimal  trajectory 

length  Ti, task  capacity  is 

bi = min (|Si|, ⌊Ti/3 ⌋). (7 )

Capacity  limits  counts,  not  spacing.  Each  task 

receives  one  scenario;  the  remaining  170  fol-

low  largest-remainder  allocation  proportional 

to  bi − 1, with  ties  by  task  ID.  Coverage  pri-

oritizes  event-ordering  or  handoff  directions, 

family/optimal-arrangement-set  combinations, 

then  material  stages.  Keys  include  all  optimal 

arrangements;  processing  progress  alone  does  not 

define  a stage. 

Ties  use  trajectory  distance,  proximity  grade, 

earlier  position,  then  candidate  ID.  Grades  order 

certified  common  optimal  prefixes  of  0,  1,  2,  or 

≥ 3 steps,  then  unknown  and  uncomputed  cases. 

Source  levels  L1–L6  contribute  10,  5,  40,  35,  50, 

and  60  scenarios;  these  are  recipe-complexity  la-

bels,  not  calibrated  item  difficulties. 

Base  questions  contain  a unique  state-optimal 

plan  and  at  least  two  feasible  alternatives.  Subopti-

mal  gaps  are  at  least  one  joint  step  for  short  goals 

and  two  for  long  goals;  short-goal  contrasts  act  be-

fore  first  attainment.  Rationales  link  state  facts  and 

program  differences  to  execution  outcomes.  Paral-

lelism  tags  require  different  productive  overlap.
