## ABSTRACT 

Model-based offline multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) provides a promising approach to multi-agent decision-making under partial observability, where the global state is unavailable from offline data. However, limited dataset coverage can lead to substantial out-of-distribution (OOD) model errors, which may mislead policy optimization and degrade downstream performance. Existing model-based offline MARL methods typically rely on access to global states or mitigate model errors through pessimistic or conservative policy optimization, leaving the reliability and refinement of learned world models less explored. We propose a Bayesian offline MARL framework that infers latent global states from decentralized observation-action histories and iteratively refines a posterior world model using only offline data. Building on posterior information loss, we extend this criterion to the multi-agent setting as multi-agent posterior information loss (MPIL), which provides a reliability measure for world model refinement and offline evaluation. Theoretically, we establish an upper bound on policy regret that is monotonic in MPIL and further characterize its dependence on the size and state–joint-action coverage of the offline dataset. Experiments on partially observ-able multi-agent variants of D4RL MuJoCo tasks demonstrate competitive policy performance across diverse offline data conditions, while MPIL-guided refinement improves world model prediction and downstream policy performance. These results support a principled connection between world model reliability, offline data coverage, and policy performance in partially observable offline MARL. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has achieved remarkable success in complex cooperative tasks [1]. However, conventional MARL typically requires extensive online interaction during training, which can incur substantial safety risks and trial-and-error costs when a sufficiently accurate simulator is unavailable. Offline MARL addresses this limitation by learning policies exclusively from historical datasets and has been widely studied in fields such as healthcare, industrial automation, and autonomous driving [2–5]. Nevertheless, in many practical partially observable multi-agent systems, the global state is inaccessible, leaving agents with only decentralized local observations. This information deficiency undermines centralized policy learning, since local observations may be insufficient to characterize the underlying environment and support reliable coordination [6]. Therefore, how to learn effective policies solely from offline data when global states are unavailable remains a fundamental challenge in offline MARL. Model-based offline RL offers a natural paradigm for fully offline policy learning and evaluation, and its core ideas have also been extended to multi-agent settings. By learning environment dynamics from offline data, a world model can serve as a surrogate environment for policy optimization and evaluation. However, limited data coverage can make the learned model unreliable, causing policy optimization to exploit model errors in out-of-distribution (OOD) regions. Existing methods mainly address this issue through pessimistic or conservative policy optimization [7–10]. While effective,

Moreover, offline RL lacks a principled criterion linking world model reliability to policy performance and safety. SOReL [11] takes an important step by introducing posterior information loss (PIL), which links world model discrepancy to policy regret, although it is limited to the single-agent setting and does not employ PIL for world model refinement. To address these challenges, we propose a latent world model framework for fully offline MARL under decentralized partial observability. The framework infers latent global states from agents’ local observation-action histories and models posterior uncertainty over transition, reward, and observation dynamics. Building on this model, we introduce multi-agent posterior information loss (MPIL) to quantify world model reliability and further integrate empirical MPIL into world model learning as a fine-tuning objective and reliability criterion. Starting from an initially trained world model, the framework iteratively refines the model until convergence, after which the resulting world model is used for policy learning and fully offline regret estimation, forming a closed loop of world model refinement, policy learning, and performance assessment without further interaction with the true environment. Within this framework, we prove that the true regret is upper-bounded by a monotonic function of MPIL, and further characterize its dependence on offline dataset size and state–joint-action coverage. These results establish a unified theoretical connection among world model reliability, offline data availability, and policy-performance guarantees. We validate the proposed theory and framework on partially observable multi-agent MuJoCo [12] benchmarks, where MPIL-guided world model refinement improves model prediction accuracy and policy performance, and the regret estimated from the refined world model follows a trend consistent with the true regret as the amount of offline data increases. 

## 2 PRELIMINARIES 

The decentralized partially observable Markov decision process (Dec-POMDP) provides a general framework for modeling cooperative multi-agent decision-making problems in which each agent has access only to local observations, while all agents share a common system-level objective [13, 14]. A Dec-POMDP is formally defined by the tuple G = ⟨I , S, {Ai} , T, R, {Zi} , {Oi} , γ ⟩. Here, 

I = {1, 2, . . . , I } denotes the finite set of agents, and S denotes the environment state space with an initial state distribution P0. At each time step t = 0 , 1, 2, . . . , the environment occupies a global state st ∈ S, and each agent i ∈ I selects an action ai,t from its action space Ai according to its policy πi. The individual actions form the joint action ˜at = ( a1,t , . . . , a I,t ). The transition function 

T (st+1 |st, ˜at) = Pr ( st+1 | st, ˜at) characterizes the stochastic evolution of the environment. The reward function is defined as R(rt|st, ˜at) = Pr( rt | st, ˜at), where the resulting global reward is shared among all agents. Under partial observability, each agent i receives a local observation zi,t 

from its observation space Zi rather than directly accessing the global state st, where the observation function Oi(zi,t +1 |st, ˜at) = Pr ( zi,t +1 | st, ˜at) characterizes the generation of local observations. The policy πi maps the local history hi,t = ( zi, 0, a i, 0, . . . , a i,t −1, z i,t ) to an action distribution 

πi(ai,t | hi,t ), and the joint policy is denoted by ˜π = ( π1, . . . , π I ). The discount factor γ ∈ [0 , 1) 

determines the relative importance of future rewards. To model partially observable multi-agent environments, we construct a dynamics model DT,R 

that jointly captures state transitions and shared rewards, together with an observation model 

˜O = {Oi}i∈I for local-observation generation. The resulting world model is defined as MWM =(DT,R , ˜O). For model learning, T and R are jointly parameterized through a transition-reward distri-bution PT,R , whereas the observation distribution are represented separately by P ˜O . ## 3 RELATED WORK 

Existing model-free offline RL methods mitigate distribution shift through policy constraints, con-servative value estimation, and pessimistic learning [15–19], with multi-agent extensions further addressing coordination through CTDE, decentralized optimization, and global-to-local regularization [20–23]. However, these methods do not explicitly model environment dynamics, limiting their ability to explicitly reason about long-horizon consequences through learned environment dynamics. Model-based offline RL provides an alternative research direction by learning environment dynamics and leveraging imagined trajectories for policy optimization. Existing methods mainly mitigate the impact of model errors on policy optimization through pessimistic modeling [7, 8, 24], uncertainty estimation [25, 26], and conservative learning [9]. Most of these methods assume that the global state required for dynamics learning is directly available, including recent model-based extensions to offline MARL [10]. In partially observable multi-agent environments, however, the global state may not be directly accessible from offline data. A common workaround is to concatenate agents’ local observations as a surrogate global representation [27, 10], while another line of work learns latent state representations from observation-action histories to capture hidden system dynamics [28–30]. Although these approaches enable dynamics modeling without direct access to the true global state, how to assess and refine the learned world model solely from fixed offline data remains underexplored. Fully offline policy assessment has been studied from several perspectives. Off-policy evaluation (OPE) estimates target-policy performance from logged data using importance weighting, value-function estimation, or learned dynamics models [31–33], while offline policy-selection methods use such estimates or alternative validation criteria to rank candidate policies and tune hyperparameters without environment interaction [34, 35]. These approaches primarily assess or select policies and value functions, rather than characterizing whether a learned world model is sufficiently reliable for downstream policy learning. More recently, SOReL [11] introduces PIL to connect posterior world model discrepancy with policy regret, enabling fully offline model assessment and tuning. However, current PIL-based approaches are mainly developed for single-agent scenarios and do not explicitly leverage PIL to update world model parameters. In this work, we develop an MPIL-guided latent world model framework for partially observable offline MARL, which infers latent system states from local observation-action histories without access to global states. MPIL is used to assess and iteratively refine the world model fully offline, while its connection to policy regret enables principled offline evaluation of multi-agent policy performance. 

## 4 METHODOLOGY 

In this section, we present a Bayesian world model-based offline MARL framework for partially observable environments, as illustrated in Fig. 1. To obtain a system-level representation for world model learning and cooperative policy optimization, the proposed framework first infers a latent global state ˆst from offline local observation-action histories and then learns a Bayesian world model. The world model is further optimized using an empirical multi-agent posterior information loss (MPIL) objective and assessed using an MPIL-based reliability criterion. Once the criterion is satisfied, the world model is used for cooperative policy optimization under the CTDE paradigm [1, 36].

## 6 EXPERIMENTS 

We adopt three classic MuJoCo continuous-control tasks from OpenAI Gym, including Halfcheetah, Hopper, and Walker2d, as the experimental environments. All experiments are conducted using the D4RL benchmark [47] for offline RL. For each task, four types of offline datasets are considered, including random, medium, full-replay, and expert, resulting in a total of 12 task-dataset combinations. To investigate offline multi-agent cooperation under partial observability, we extend each original single-agent dataset to a multi-agent setting. Each agent has access only to its local observations, while the joint actions of all agents collectively determine the environment dynamics. To ensure a consistent information setting across different methods, the global state is excluded from the offline datasets and remains unavailable to all methods throughout training. 6.1 OFFLINE POLICY PERFORMANCE 

Policy performance is evaluated in terms of normalized average return and compared with behavior cloning (BC), OMAR [21], and MOMA-PPO [10]. Once the world model satisfies the MPIL criterion, we fix the model posterior and generate imagined trajectories for cooperative multi-agent policy optimization under the CTDE paradigm. Table 1 reports the normalized returns across the three tasks and four dataset types, using the standard D4RL reference scores. Results are reported as mean ± standard deviation over 5 random seeds, with each final policy evaluated over 10 episodes. Implementation details and hyperparameter settings for all methods are provided in Appendix A.2. Overall, the proposed method demonstrates strong and consistent policy performance across different tasks and dataset types, achieving the highest normalized return in eight of the twelve experimental settings and ranking second in the remaining four, with particularly clear advantages on the random and full-replay datasets. In comparison, BC performs well on the expert datasets of Hopper and Walker2d but degrades substantially on the random and some full-replay datasets, reflecting its strong dependence on the quality of the offline behavior data. MOMA-PPO achieves the best performance on the Halfcheetah full-replay and medium datasets, but its performance is less consistent across the other tasks. OMAR also exhibits noticeable performance variation across environments and yields relatively low returns in most of the settings considered. These results indicate that the proposed method adapts well to variations in offline data quality and coverage, and can extract decision-relevant information from offline trajectories rather than relying primarily on high-quality behavior demonstrations. 7Preprint, October 2026. 6.2 VALIDATION OF MPIL AND APPROXIMATE REGRET 

To validate the proposed offline evaluation mechanism, we jointly analyze δMPIL and approximate regret across different offline dataset sizes. As shown in Fig. 2, both 

δMPIL and approximate regret decrease substantially at the early stage with increasing offline data in the Walker2d environment. This indicates that, as the pos-terior world model becomes more accurate, the down-stream policy learned from it also exhibits improved performance. Once the dataset size reaches approxi-mately 0.5M, both metrics gradually stabilize, suggest-ing that the information provided by additional offline data yields only limited further improvement to either the world model or the learned policy. Moreover, the ap-proximate regret follows a trend consistent with the true regret and stabilizes at a similar data scale, supporting its validity as an offline proxy for policy performance. The consistent stabilization of δMPIL , approximate re-gret, and true regret further provides a practical indica-tion of data sufficiency, allowing the data scale required for stable policy performance to be estimated without online interaction.

To further examine the effect of MPIL-guided world model refinement, we compare the predictive performance of the world models and their corresponding downstream policies before and after refinement under the same data budget. For each of the four dataset types, the baseline model is trained directly on the full set of 0.5M samples, whereas the refined model is initialized with a subset and progressively updated with additional samples according to the MPIL criterion. Both models are evaluated on the corresponding validation set and on a mixed validation set constructed from all four dataset types. As an additional reference, we also train a separate world model on 0.5M samples from the mixed dataset and evaluate it using the same validation settings. World model prediction performance is measured by the joint normalized root mean square error (JNRMSE), which jointly characterizes observation and reward prediction errors. Since simulator global states are excluded and not predicted, state prediction error is omitted. As shown in Fig. 3a, MPIL-guided refinement in the Walker2d environment generally reduces the prediction error of the world models trained on individual datasets. The improvement is particularly evident on the mixed validation set, where the initial models exhibit substantially larger errors under the shifted data distribution. After refinement, their JNRMSE values are markedly reduced and are generally lower than those of the reference model trained directly on 0.5M mixed samples. These 8Preprint, October 2026. results indicate that MPIL-guided refinement can utilize a fixed amount of offline data more effectively, yielding more accurate and robust world model predictions across different data distributions. The benefits of world model refinement are further reflected in policy learning. As shown in Fig. 3b, across all four Walker2d datasets, policies trained with the refined world models achieve higher returns, with the improvement being particularly pronounced on the full-replay dataset. This indicates that improved world model predictions can provide more reliable imagined trajectories and thereby enhance offline policy optimization.

## 7 CONCLUSION 

This paper studies fully offline MARL under decentralized partial observability, where the global state is unavailable during training. We develop a Bayesian world model approach that infers latent states from local observation histories and models transition-reward and observation dynamics through posterior distributions. MPIL is further introduced to refinement of the posterior world model and enable fully offline evaluation, while posterior-consistent imagined trajectories are used to optimize cooperative multi-agent policies. Theoretical analysis establishes a monotone upper bound on policy regret in terms of MPIL and further characterizes its dependence on the size and coverage of the offline dataset. Experiments on partially observable multi-agent MuJoCo tasks show that the proposed method achieves competitive policy performance across datasets of varying quality. MPIL decreases consistently with policy regret as offline information increases, while MPIL-guided refinement further improves world model prediction and downstream policy performance. Future work will investigate zero-shot generalization to unseen agent configurations, interaction structures, and environment dynamics. We will also explore shared or conditioned world models that can reuse learned representations and dynamics across different task settings rather than retraining a new model from scratch. When model refinement is required, MPIL may further be used to identify unreliable regions and select the most informative offline samples for targeted updates, improving the data efficiency of world model refinement. We evaluate next-observation and reward predictions on a held-out test set. To further examine whether a reduction in MPIL corresponds to more accurate modeling of the environment dynamics, we compare the next-observation and reward predictions of the initial world model with those of the world model optimized under MPIL guidance on the Walker2d task. As shown in Figure 4, the dashed line y = x represents ideal predictions, where the model outputs exactly match the ground-truth. For next-observation prediction, although the initial world model generally captures the trends across different observation dimensions, noticeable prediction dispersion remains in some regions. After the MPIL-guided posterior updates, the predictions across observation dimensions become more concentrated around y = x, with substantially reduced dispersion. A similar trend is observed for reward prediction. These results are consistent with the decreasing trend of MPIL during training, indicating that lower MPIL reflects improved characterization of next-observation and reward dynamics by the world model, thereby providing a more reliable model foundation for subsequent policy learning based on imagined trajectories.
